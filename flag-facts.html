A few things worth knowing before your next round — some of these show up as genuinely useful tells once you know to look for them.

The one flag that isn't a rectangle

Nepal

Every national flag in the world is a rectangle except one. Nepal's flag is made of two stacked pennant shapes (a crimson double-triangle), each bearing a small white moon and sun. It's the only non-rectangular national flag on Earth, a rule so specific that Nepal's constitution actually defines the flag's geometry with mathematical construction lines.

Perfectly square flags

Switzerland & Vatican City

Almost every flag is wider than it is tall, but two countries buck the trend entirely: Switzerland and Vatican City both fly perfectly square flags, a 1:1 ratio. Switzerland's white cross on red is also one of the oldest continuously used national flag designs in the world.

Color symbolism shows up in patterns

Pan-African colors

Red, gold (or yellow), and green appear together on a striking number of African flags — Ethiopia's flag was one of the earliest to use this combination, and many nations that gained independence later chose the same palette deliberately, as a visual link to Pan-African identity rather than coincidence.

Pan-Arab colors

Similarly, red, white, black, and green turn up repeatedly across the Arab world (Egypt, Iraq, Syria, Yemen, Jordan, and others), tracing back to a shared set of colors historically associated with different Arab dynasties and later adopted as a symbol of Arab unity.

The Nordic cross

Sweden, Norway, Denmark, Finland, and Iceland all share the same basic design: an off-center cross reaching to all four edges. It originates from Denmark's flag, considered one of the oldest state flags still in use, with the design spreading to its neighbors over centuries.

Flags that are easy to mix up

A few pairs are famous for tripping people up, since they share the same colors in a similar layout:

  • Indonesia vs. Poland — both are simple two-band flags in red and white, just in opposite order (Indonesia is red-over-white; Poland is white-over-red).
  • Chad vs. Romania — nearly identical vertical blue-yellow-red tricolors, differing only in a slightly different shade of blue.
  • Ireland vs. Ivory Coast — both are green-white-orange, but reversed: Ireland is green-white-orange, Ivory Coast is orange-white-green.

A flag with two circles

South Korea

The taegeuk (the red-and-blue circle at the center of South Korea's flag) is a balance symbol with roots in centuries-old East Asian philosophy, and the four black trigrams in the corners each represent a different classical element and season.
