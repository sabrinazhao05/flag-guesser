Why this exists

Flag Guesser started as a simple question: how many of the world's 195 flags could I actually recognize on sight? It grew from a quick quiz idea into a full game with adjustable difficulty, multiple ways to answer, timed rounds, and a real-time versus mode so two people on completely different devices can race to name the same flag.

What's inside

Every flag in the game is drawn as vector artwork rather than a photograph, so it loads instantly and never depends on an outside image service. The full roster covers all 193 United Nations member states plus Vatican City and Palestine, sorted into Easy, Medium, and Hard tiers based on general worldwide familiarity.

You can play solo — as a quick 10-flag round, a longer 25 or 50, an "every flag" marathon, or an endless streak that ends the moment you miss one — or challenge a friend directly in Versus mode, where you both see the same flag at the same time and race to answer first.

How it's built

This is a single self-contained web page: no app store, no install, no account required to play solo. Versus mode connects two browsers directly to each other using WebRTC, with a small relay service stepping in only when a direct connection isn't possible across different networks.

A quick note on accuracy

Most flags here are drawn to match their real proportions and colors closely. A handful of especially intricate designs (elaborate coats of arms, detailed emblems) are simplified to a recognizable stand-in rather than reproduced with heraldic precision — the goal is a fair, fun guessing game, not a vexillology reference.
